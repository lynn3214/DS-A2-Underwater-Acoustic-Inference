# Draft course-platform summary (English, 150–300 words)

This project estimates the within-date difference between nighttime and daytime published hourly sound-level indicators at NOAA SanctSound station SB02_13. After converting UTC start-of-bin timestamps to America/New_York and applying the pre-specified requirement of at least six valid hours in each window, 64 eligible dates remained. The estimated mean night-minus-day difference was 0.352 dB (median 0.635 dB; sample SD 3.123 dB). The IID one-sample t 95% confidence interval was −0.428 to 1.132 dB (two-sided p=0.370). The Newey–West HAC interval was −0.564 to 1.268 dB; moving-block bootstrap intervals also included zero for block lengths 2, 4, and 8. Thus these data do not provide evidence of a nonzero average contrast under the specified analyses. The point estimate is below the operational 1 dB and 3 dB interpretation thresholds; the intervals include 1 dB but exclude 3 dB.

In an IID Gaussian simulation calibrated to the observed marginal standard deviation, analytical and Monte Carlo power were about 0.999 at n=30 for a 3 dB effect, but fell to about 0.713 when noise doubled. A stationary AR(1) counterexample showed substantial size distortion: at rho=0.6, null rejection rates were 0.327 for IID t, 0.156 for HAC, and 0.175 for MBB. Dependence-aware methods reduced but did not remove the distortion. Results are limited to one short deployment; record-level QC is unavailable, and NOAA product metadata disagree on whether the lower frequency limit is 10 or 20 Hz. This observational analysis supports no causal claim.
